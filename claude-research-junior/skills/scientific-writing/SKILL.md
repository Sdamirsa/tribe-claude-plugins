# Scientific Writing

This skill defines a scientific writing style that combines philosophical rigor with accessible prose. The goal is writing that reads as a coherent argument where each sentence earns its place.

## Core Philosophy

Write with Shakespeare's narrative flow, Einstein's curiosity, and Wolfram's systematic thinking. Scientific writing is philosophy made operational. Start from the big picture and work downstream to specifics, maintaining alignment with the paper's scope throughout.

## Intellectual Standards

**Honesty over impression.** Never exaggerate findings or oversimplify complexity. State what the evidence supports, acknowledge what remains uncertain, and explain limitations as part of the scientific narrative rather than as obligatory disclaimers.

**Hedging with purpose.** Prefer hedged language that reflects evidence strength ("may suggest," "appears to," "evidence indicates") over false certainty. Match confidence to the data. When evidence is strong, say so clearly.

**Conflicts deserve attention.** When literature conflicts or findings are ambiguous, engage with this honestly. Conflicting evidence is not a problem to hide but a feature of honest science.

**Scope alignment.** Every paragraph should serve the paper's central argument. Before writing, know what the paper claims. While writing, ensure each section advances that claim.

## Paragraph Architecture

Structure paragraphs like stepping stones across a lake. Each stone is a piece of logic or evidence from prior work. Each step must be grounded before moving to the next.

**Opening sentence:** Connect to the previous paragraph while previewing this paragraph's contribution. This sentence bridges and broadens.

**Body sentences:** Expand the opening claim with evidence, reasoning, or necessary context. Each sentence should follow logically from the previous one.

**Closing sentence:** Either conclude the paragraph's argument or transition toward the next idea. The reader should feel the path forward.

## Sentence-Level Principles

**Active voice.** Prefer "We analyzed" over "Data were analyzed." Active voice clarifies who did what and strengthens the narrative.

**Concision without loss.** Remove words that add no meaning. Short sentences are fine. Long sentences are fine if every word works. Avoid wordiness, but do not sacrifice precision for brevity.

**Flow between sentences.** Each sentence should connect to the previous one. Use logical connectors, repeated key terms, or pronoun references to maintain continuity. The reader should never wonder how two adjacent sentences relate.

## Forbidden Elements

These break the style and must never appear in scientific prose:

**No em dashes or en dashes.** Use commas, parentheses, or restructure the sentence instead.

**No colons to break sentences.** Do not write "There is one key finding: the model failed." Restructure as "The key finding is that the model failed" or use two sentences.

**No bullet points or numbered lists.** Scientific prose flows as paragraphs. Convert any list into integrated sentences. Instead of listing three limitations, write "The study has three main limitations. First, the sample..." and continue in prose.

## Document-Specific Guidance

**Abstracts:** Begin with the problem's significance, state the gap, describe the approach, report key findings, and end with implications. Every sentence must count. Maintain narrative flow even in 250 words.

**Introduction sections:** Move from broad context to specific gap to your contribution. The final paragraph should state exactly what the paper does and preview its structure.

**Methods sections:** Describe what was done with enough detail for replication. Organize by logical phases of work, not by chronology of how you happened to do it.

**Results sections:** Present findings in the order that builds the argument, not the order you discovered them. Integrate interpretation lightly to guide the reader.

**Discussion sections:** Begin with the main finding and its meaning. Situate within existing literature. Address limitations as part of the scientific narrative. End with implications and future directions.

**Literature reviews:** Build an argument, not a list. Each paragraph should advance a point about the field. Synthesize across papers rather than summarizing them sequentially.

## Figures and Tables

Every table and figure must have a clear title. Figure legends should provide concise descriptions that state what the figure shows, referencing sub-panels (a, b, c) as needed to guide the reader through the visual. Keep legends purely descriptive. Do not include interpretive statements, discussion, or conclusions in figure or table legends.

## Quality Check

Before finalizing, verify that every paragraph serves the paper's central claim, that a reader can follow the logic from any sentence to the next, that limitations and uncertainties are stated honestly, that the prose contains no dashes, sentence-breaking colons, or bullet points, and that the writing would fit in npj Digital Medicine or a comparable venue.
